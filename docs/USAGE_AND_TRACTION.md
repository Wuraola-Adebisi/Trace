## 2. Early user feedback

The following are concise summaries of feedback supplied by the founder. They are not direct quotations and should not be placed in quotation marks or presented as verbatim testimonials.

### Demola — meeting comprehension and accountability

Demola reported that Trace helped him understand his meeting notes more clearly, identify stakeholders, understand who was responsible for what, and see how many items needed to be tracked.

**Product signal:** Users may value Trace not only for summarising meeting notes, but for making responsibilities and follow-up work easier to understand.

### Sarah — confidence after meetings

Sarah reported feeling more confident attending meetings because she knows that, if she misses details, she can share her notes with Trace afterwards to help make sense of the discussion and recover important details.

**Product signal:** Trace may reduce the perceived risk of missing information during meetings and help users review notes afterwards.

### Seven additional feedback contributors

The founder reports receiving further positive feedback from seven other people. Individual names, exact wording, dates, use cases and supporting records have not yet been supplied. These should be added only when confirmed.

**Product signal:** There is early positive qualitative reception beyond the two feedback examples above. The themes and strength of that feedback still need to be documented individually.

## 3. What the feedback suggests

The early feedback points to three hypotheses worth validating:

1. **Comprehension:** Trace helps people make sense of dense or disorganised meeting notes.
2. **Accountability:** Trace helps people identify stakeholders, responsibilities and follow-up items.
3. **Confidence and recall:** Trace helps people recover details after meetings and feel less concerned about missing information.

These are promising user-value signals, not yet measured outcome claims. The next pilot should test whether users can complete these tasks more accurately or quickly with Trace than without it.

## 4. Initial tester programme

Recruit a small, permissioned cohort of 10–15 testers for a focused validation round. Prioritise people who regularly attend meetings and manage projects:

- Founders and startup operators.
- Project and product managers.
- Team leads and coordinators.
- People responsible for meeting notes and follow-up.

### Pilot task

Ask each tester to use a real meeting note or project document they are authorised to share. They should:

1. Submit the document to Trace.
2. Review the people, topics, decisions, questions and relationships surfaced.
3. Identify responsibilities and follow-up items.
4. Compare Trace's output with what they already understood from the source.
5. Report missing, incorrect or confusing information.
6. Return for a second use with another document, where practical.

Do not ask testers to upload confidential, personal, regulated or employer-owned material unless they have permission and the product's data handling is appropriate for it. Use redacted or synthetic documents when necessary.

### Questions to ask after each session

- What were you trying to understand or accomplish?
- What did Trace help you notice that you might otherwise have missed?
- Which extracted items were incorrect or incomplete?
- Were responsibilities and follow-up actions clear?
- Could you trace an insight back to the original note?
- What did you still have to do manually?
- Would you use Trace for your next meeting or project document? Why?
- What would prevent you from using it again?

## 5. Usage and retention definitions

Use consistent definitions before publishing any metrics.

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Registered user | A user account successfully created |
| Activated user | A user who successfully processes at least one document and views its results |
| Active user | A user who performs at least one meaningful product action in the reporting period |
| Returning user | An activated user who performs a meaningful action on a later day |
| Retained user | An activated user who is active in a defined later cohort window |
| Processed document | A document for which processing completed successfully |
| Engaged workspace | A workspace with meaningful activity by at least one member |
| Evidence interaction | A user opens or inspects a source reference supporting an extracted item or answer |

Report cumulative users separately from daily, weekly and monthly active users. Define retention windows (for example, day 7 and day 30) and report the cohort denominator.

## 6. Evidence collection and reporting

For each pilot participant, record:

- Pseudonymous participant ID.
- Role or user segment, only where voluntarily provided.
- First-use date and follow-up date.
- Document type and approximate length; avoid retaining document content in the feedback log.
- Whether processing completed.
- Whether the user inspected results and source references.
- Task outcome and observed friction.
- Feedback, correction and requested improvement.
- Permission status for attribution or public use of feedback.

Do not publish a person's name, employer, document, screenshot or testimonial without explicit permission. Keep raw notes and identifiable contact details separate from public-facing summaries.

## 7. Improvement log

For every product change informed by user feedback, record:

| Date | Feedback / observed issue | Product change | Status | Follow-up result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| To be recorded | To be recorded | To be recorded | Not yet recorded | Not yet measured |

Do not claim an improvement was made until the relevant code or product change is shipped. Do not claim an improvement worked until it has been checked with users or usage evidence.

## 8. Next reporting milestone

The next useful traction update should include:

- A dated analytics snapshot supporting the cumulative user count.
- Activated users and successful document processing count.
- Returning users and day-7/day-30 retention, once cohorts mature.
- A small set of permissioned, documented use cases.
- Feedback themes, corrections and shipped improvements.
- At least one follow-up session with users who have already tried Trace.

The aim is not to manufacture impressive numbers. It is to show that real people have tried Trace, identify the value they get from it, and demonstrate that their feedback is shaping the product.
